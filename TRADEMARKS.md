# PromeTorch Trademark Policy

The name **"PromeTorch"** and the PromeTorch logo (when published) are
trademarks of the PromeTorch project. The Apache 2.0 license under which
PromeTorch is distributed gives you broad rights to **use, modify, and
redistribute** the code — but it does **not** automatically grant rights
to use the "PromeTorch" name or logo for promotion of derived products.

This document defines what's allowed and what's not.

---

## ✅ Always allowed (no permission needed)

- **Use** PromeTorch in your application, research, product, or service.
- **Modify** PromeTorch source code for your own needs.
- **Distribute** modified versions, provided you follow Apache 2.0
  (keep copyright notice, NOTICE file, etc.).
- **Reference** PromeTorch by name in:
  - Academic papers, blog posts, documentation
  - Comparison tables and benchmarks
  - "Powered by PromeTorch" / "Built with PromeTorch" attribution
  - Talks, presentations, social media posts
- **Fork** the repository on GitHub or any other VCS host.
- **Charge for** services around PromeTorch:
  - Consulting / training / support
  - Hosting PromeTorch-based inference / training
  - Selling hardware that runs PromeTorch
  - Commercial applications that internally use PromeTorch

## ⚠️ Requires written permission

- **Rebranding** PromeTorch as your own product and selling it as
  "core deep-learning framework" (e.g. "Foobar ML Framework, powered by
  PromeTorch" with no mention of PromeTorch elsewhere). This is
  considered trademark dilution.
- **Using "PromeTorch" in your product name** (e.g. "PromeTorch Enterprise",
  "PromeTorch Pro", "PromeTorch.io"). The name "PromeTorch" alone or
  with modifiers is reserved.
- **Using a confusingly similar name** ("PromTorch", "PrometheusTorch",
  "Promethorch" without the 'h') that could mislead users about origin.
- **Using the PromeTorch logo** in promotional materials for non-PromeTorch
  products without modification or attribution.

## ❌ Not allowed

- **Implying endorsement** by the PromeTorch project of your product
  ("Official PromeTorch partner", "Recommended by PromeTorch") without
  written agreement.
- **Registering "PromeTorch" or confusingly similar marks** in your own
  name, in any jurisdiction.
- **Using "PromeTorch" for products that compete with PromeTorch itself**
  (i.e. selling a rebranded version as a deep-learning framework).

---

## How to request permission

For any of the "requires written permission" cases above, open a GitHub
issue in the main repository titled **`[trademark] <your request>`** or
contact the maintainers directly.

Reasonable requests will get a yes. The point of this policy is **not**
to prevent commercial use of PromeTorch — it's to prevent ambiguity
about what's "official PromeTorch" vs derivatives.

---

## Why this policy exists

Open-source projects that don't have a trademark policy run into one
of two failure modes:

1. **Confusion:** Multiple "PromeTorch" forks appear with different
   APIs. Users don't know which is "the real one." Bug reports go to the
   wrong project. Trust in the brand erodes.

2. **Free riding:** A company takes the code, slaps their logo on it,
   sells it as their own work, and the original community gets no
   credit or contributions back.

The combination of **Apache 2.0 license** (for the code) + **trademark
policy** (for the name) is the standard adopted by major open-source
projects like Kubernetes, TensorFlow, Apache Spark, and Linux. It gives
maximum freedom to use the code while preserving the integrity of the
project identity.
